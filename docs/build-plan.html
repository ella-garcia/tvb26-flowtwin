Core platform build plan · v5 · early warning

FlowTwin build plan

An early-warning platform for automotive supply chains in Mexico. A Tier 1 supplier pays for it and invites its Tier 2 suppliers for free. Each supplier gets a risk traffic light that predicts delivery problems before they stop the line, for example a supplier near Veracruz in rainy season whose transit goes from 2 to 5 days. A digital twin of each supplier's network projects the next 14 days and tests whether it can absorb the 15% demand swings contracts allow.

7 October 2026FlowTwin is a working nameStatus: v0 in buildNew marks items added in v5
Why v5 changed

A market scan (gap analysis) confirmed no one predicts per-part line stops for Mexican Tier 2s, but showed the plan's signals were thin. Trade and tariff risk and upstream component shortages were missing, and most Mexican feeds have no open API. An integrations plan replaces upload-only intake with an EDI copy, one ERP connector and e-invoices (CFDI). WhatsApp becomes a send-only alert channel with a one-tap reply page. Predicted vs actual moves from Phase 6 to Phase 2.

Costs and margins are never sharedA Tier 1 sees risk, stock, flex capacity and delivery performance. Supplier prices and costs are dropped when data arrives, whether from ERP, EDI, invoices or a typed reply, not just hidden in the UI.
Suppliers see what their customer seesEach Tier 2 sees its own traffic light, the reasons behind it and every alert about it.
Rank by line-stop risk, not by spendA MX$2 clip with no substitute can stop a line. Every list sorts by days to line stop and exposure.

How the pieces fit

Signals and the twin feed the traffic light

External signals and each supplier's own data feed a twin that projects the next 14 days. The risk engine compares projected delivery times, and the supplier's own input supply, with the Tier 1's stock of each part and raises an alert before cover runs out. In v0 the projection is pre-computed by a standalone Monte Carlo model; ISOMORPH replaces it in Phase 2. Every data source writes through the same intake checks as an upload.

Before we go further

Risks and open decisions

Biggest open questions

Will a Tier 1 share its stock per part and its supplier list with us? The early warning depends on knowing how many days of cover the Tier 1 holds. In the same first conversation, ask which ERP and EDI provider it uses and whether its IT will approve a read-only connection and a copy of its 830/862/856 traffic. That sets the order of the Phase 2 intake work.

Open itemWhy it matters
Line-stop cost of €15k per minuteFrom the advisor call. Published figures vary widely; confirm with a Tier 1 and its chargeback terms
ISOMORPH adaptationStill the main technical risk. A zero-shot forecasting baseline for thin history lowers it
Road closure and blockade dataThe second technical risk: no open API, so it needs a Spanish news and social parser or paid traffic data
Mexican-side border and port dataCBP covers only the US side; no public ANAM or ASIPONA feed. Needs a broker partner or AIS data
Holding a supplier's e.firmaIt is legally a signature. Use Syntage so FlowTwin never holds it; get Mexican legal advice before direct SAT downloads
People reply in WhatsApp anywayAnswers in the chat would be lost. Every template says "Responde en el enlace"; track how often it happens and revisit send-only if it's frequent
Reply links without loginsA forwarded link lets someone else answer. Tokens cover one alert, expire after 72 hours and are logged
Language of the UIBRIEF.md says English; suppliers work in Spanish. Proposed: Spanish-first supplier screens and messages, English Tier 1 screens for now
LFPDPPP 2025FlowTwin has direct obligations for contact data: privacy notice, consent log, retention, rights handling. Meta processes WhatsApp messages and must be named
Competitors closing inOverhaul (with FreightVerify), Prewave's free supplier network and Resilinc are the most likely to move into part-level line-stop prediction
