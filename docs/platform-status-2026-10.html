1. Platform today

FlowTwin is a React app on Supabase (Postgres with row-level sharing rules) plus a Python worker that computes risk, parses uploads, pulls weather signals and sends alert emails. It runs on seed data or live against Supabase. A yellow testing bar switches role and company for demos.

User journeys

Key customer: spot and act on a risk

  1. Open the risk board: 2 suppliers at Act now, soonest line stop in 2 days.
  2. Pick K3 compact SUV: its line stops in 2 days. Pick M5 midsize sedan: no stop expected.
  3. Open Parts & stock: 7 parts run out before their next delivery, 5 of them line stoppers.
  4. Open Hules y Mangueras de Orizaba: rain pushes transit from 2 to 5 days; 3 days of cover on a line-stopper part; fails the +15% test.
  5. Open Polímeros Frontera (Laredo, Texas): a customs outage at Nuevo Laredo takes transit from 4 to about 7 days; the route card shows the days piling up at the border and customs.
  6. In Alerts, choose “Pull the next order forward” and acknowledge.

Supplier: see what the customer sees

  1. Receive a free, sponsored invite from the Tier 1.
  2. Open My risk: the same light and reasons the customer sees; costs and margins never shared.
  3. Answer an alert and confirm capacity.
  4. Fill My data (capacity, lead times, stock) so the score uses measured data.

Key customer: connect real data

  1. Download a template on the Data page.
  2. Upload an Excel or CSV export (CONTPAQi-style Spanish headers work).
  3. Fix flagged rows; the worker recomputes risk automatically.

Admin: run the signals

  1. Live rain forecasts arrive hourly (Open-Meteo; SMN optional).
  2. Switch a signal off when it is wrong; risk is recomputed without it.

Features

Risk engine

  • 14-day projection: Monte Carlo of transit time with active signals applied.
  • 12-week outlook: weekly High / Watch / OK from seasonal and announced signals (estimated).
  • Line stop: first day a line-stopper or high part runs out, per part and per model.
  • Route legs for imports: border, customs and port times; each signal slows only the legs it reaches.
  • +15% flex test: can the supplier absorb the contract demand swing.
  • Score 0–100 from drivers; red at 65 or a stop within 3 days.

Stock and models

  • Pipeline per part: here, on the road, at the supplier.
  • Next delivery: confirmed date or estimate, labelled “Estimated”.
  • Vehicle models: each part mapped to the models it goes into.

Supplier performance

  • OTIF over 12 weeks with trend.
  • Delivery grade A/B/C against the 98% contract target.
  • Data status: connected, invited, public data only.

Platform

  • Sharing rules enforced in the database for every table.
  • Worker: FastAPI on Vercel (Docker for Cloud Run), hourly cron.
  • Alert emails through Resend (dry run without a key).
  • Calculation panel on every important figure.

2. Decisions taken

Money is never shown; the €15k per minute stays internal

The setting still exists and the engine uses it to pick the most exposed part, but no screen, alert or email shows euros.

Why: published line-stop costs vary 10–20×, the figure belongs to the OEM and a Tier 1 cannot validate it; the industry speaks in days of stock.

Rank by time to line stop, then part criticality, then score

Never by spend or value. A two-peso clip can stop a line.

Delivery record is a separate axis from the risk light

Grade A at or above the contract OTIF target (98%), B up to 3 points below, C further below. The risk light looks forward; the grade looks back.

Why: it tells “red because of the weather” apart from “a supplier that is often late”.

“Below safe cover” means stock runs out before the next delivery

One rule shared by the board, the parts page and the supplier page. Deliveries land on whole days; an estimated date is labelled “Estimated”.

Unknown is shown as unknown, never as zero

In-transit units and supplier stock come only from connected suppliers. Others show “Unknown” or “Not shared”.

Vehicle models use fictional OEM names

OEM A, B and C with models K3, M5, T1 and C2. No real brands appear in the product. The line stop for a model comes from the engine’s stop day for each of that model’s parts.

Each step is its own commit on a feature branch

A, B and C, the refactor plan and each refactor package are separate commits on professor-feedback, not on main.

Imports are modelled as route legs; domestic suppliers are untouched

A supplier’s route is a list of legs with normal times. A customs outage slows only border and customs legs; a road closure only road legs. Suppliers without a route keep the single road leg, and their numbers did not change.

Why: customs is a control point for imported material, and the delay has to show where it happens, not as a vague “transit is longer”.

The Nuevo Laredo case is fictional but modelled on real reports

Polímeros Frontera (Laredo, Texas) and the outage signal are demo data, sourced to press reports of ANAM system outages in the evidence brief.

Two horizons: 14 days for alerts, 12 weeks for planning

Following BCG’s example of flagging risks about 12 weeks ahead, FlowTwin adds a weekly 12-week outlook built only from signals known in advance (rainy and hurricane season, year-end border peaks, holiday traffic). Alerts and line stops still come from the 14-day daily projection.

Why: weather, road and customs events can only be forecast about two weeks out; beyond that, only seasonal and announced risks are known, and the page says so.

What-if results come from the engine, not the browser

The engine computes every combination of each supplier’s recommended actions (at most 8) with the outlook rule; the page only picks and counts them. The “no action” result equals the outlook exactly.

Why: one risk engine, so the before and after a Tier 1 sees are always consistent with the rest of the product.

Money only on What-if, never on the risk board

The risk board shows an optimization score, the benefit of each lever and line uptime %. Revenue lost or protected and the cost of added stock appear only on What-if, in MXN, from the Tier 1’s own unit costs and content value per vehicle, marked estimated.

Why: the professor asked for impact without money on the board; the Tier 1 still needs a business view when it plans actions.

An Industry view next to the Operational score

A toggle shows how an automaker or a bank would read the same suppliers: every supplier counts, structural weaknesses set a floor (single source, near capacity, dependence on one customer, imported inputs, no data, grade C, failed +15% test), optimizations work with 75% probability and show a range, and any expected line stop caps the score at Poor. Demo: 80 → 95 operational, 59 → 76 industry.

Why: the operational score only sees announced risks at at-risk suppliers, so it reads higher than an outside reviewer would.

Refactor before ports and customs

The risk engine existed twice (seed generator and worker). D changes the engine, so it was unified first.

Open items

  • After D: let the engine use in-transit stock and confirmed delivery dates; add a sea-freight import (e.g. via Manzanillo); let suppliers or the Tier 1 edit routes in the app.
  • Decision: customers can see a supplier’s utilisation and bottleneck even when it shares public data only.
  • Before real data: switch testing mode off (today any visitor can take any role).
  • Calibrate the scores: the High/Watch weights, the Good/Fair/Poor bands, and the Industry view parameters (75% execution, 0.5-day cap at 59, 85% capacity and 40% of sales thresholds) against a real automaker or bank scorecard.
  • Decision: the Industry view shows each supplier’s share of sales to the customer; confirm suppliers agree to share it.
  • Not yet scored: financial health (needs supplier consent and financial data), quality (defect rate, certification status), and a base rate for unannounced disruptions.
  • Costs not in the data: freight per route, customs broker fees and second-source qualification, so those optimizations show as “not costed” on What-if.
  • Demo reset: any signed-in user can reset while testing mode is on, and upload records are not cleared by a reset.
  • Later: generate database types from one source; a few components still read data directly instead of taking props.
