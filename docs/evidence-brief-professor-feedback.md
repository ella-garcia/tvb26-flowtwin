# Evidence brief: professor feedback on FlowTwin v0

*Appendix · compiled 6 Oct 2026 · FlowTwin v0 (Tier 1 early warning, Mexico)*

The professor's review of the v0 prototype made five points. This page records public evidence for each one. **Strength** reflects source quality: news and peer-reviewed sources rate higher than vendor marketing.

| # | Professor's point | Evidence | Strength |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Remove the € exposure figure.** The impact doesn't need a money value. | Published line-stop costs vary 10–20×: about $22k/min for OEM assembly lines vs $900–2,100/min for a US Tier 1 [1]. The €15k figure is the OEM's cost, and a Tier 1 user can't validate it. In the Nexperia crisis, industry warnings were given in time ("stocks will last only a few weeks"), not euros [2]. | Medium (indirect) |
| 2 | **Stock is key; one supplier supplies many parts.** | Honda stopped its Celaya, Gto. plant on 28 Oct 2025 once Nexperia chip stock ran out [3]. One supplier, many part numbers: risk sits at the part level. Peer-reviewed automotive risk models score **part** and **supplier** risk separately [4]. | High |
| 3 | **Make clear it's a Tier 1 tool; show the customer and the vehicle model.** | Tier 1 seating plants in Mexico build for several models at once, e.g. Lear Ramos Arizpe: Chevrolet Cruze and Equinox [5]. Adient Lerma is a just-in-time plant for several OEMs [6]. Disruptions are reported per model (Celaya: HR-V) [3]. | High |
| 4 | **Include ports and customs transit times.** | Nuevo Laredo: ANAM suspended cargo operations at Bridge III after system failures. It handles about 40% of Mexico's foreign trade. Queues reached 48 km and press warned plants could stop for lack of inputs (date to confirm) [7]. Manzanillo: a stoppage in May 2025 caused about $150M in losses, and customs dwell kept costs up to 20% higher for months after [8]. July 2023: Altamira delays of 15 days for 3+ months, and Toyota looked for other ports [9]. Imports of auto parts come about 49% from the US and 16% from China [10]. | High |
| 5 | **Show each supplier's historical delivery performance as a risk factor.** | OTIF is a core automotive scorecard KPI; delivery weighs about 30% in a typical scorecard [11]. Falling OTIF often comes weeks or months before a major disruption, and vendors now predict risk from delivery history [12]. | Medium (vendor sources) |

**Takeaways.** Points 2–4 are backed by real Mexican cases that work for the demo (Celaya, Nuevo Laredo, Manzanillo). Point 5 matches standard industry practice. For point 1, keep the cost setting internally but don't show it.

**Caveat.** Sources [5], [9] and [3] (Adnkronos) were read in full; the others come from search summaries. Check each one before citing it formally.
